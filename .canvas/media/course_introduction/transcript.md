# CSC-244 course introduction transcript

## Narration

Welcome to CSC-244, Android Development With Kotlin, taught by Professor Harris Williams at Bunker Hill Community College. You'll turn your programming experience into apps people can use.

This course is fully online and asynchronous, with one module every week. You'll build interfaces, connect front ends to back ends, and explore authentication, security, publishing, and monetization. You'll prototype screens for touch and keyboard input, use GET and POST requests, manage permissions, and plan transfers around connectivity and battery life. Here's how your skills will grow.

Kotlin Basics introduces decisions, loops, collections, and classes. In Creating Your First App, you'll set up Android Studio and run an app. Working With the UI Editor connects screen controls to your Kotlin code.

Next, Activities and Intents covers navigation, lifecycle changes, and Logcat debugging. UI in Depth adds styles, drawables, and animation. Menus and Lists helps you organize actions and repeated items.

Saving and Loading Data keeps information available when an app reopens. Background Tasks explores responsive interfaces. Then Volley introduces requests that bring web API data into your app.

Notifications helps you create useful alerts, while Camera covers taking and saving photos. In Module 12, Work on the Note-Taking App Project brings your skills together in a Note-Taking App connected to a REST API.

Deployment explores publishing and your final-project proposal and prototype in Module 13. Beyond Android Studio compares backend services and other mobile approaches. Module 15, Work on the Final Project, gives you room to create an app that solves a problem you choose.

So, what does a week look like? Review the slides, watch the videos, read, and practice. Each module has a graded discussion about real-world apps. Assignments are not required for every module; the assigned app mini-projects grow in complexity.

Now let's find your materials. In Canvas, select Modules in the course menu. Learning Materials contains your preparation; Deliverables contains the graded work. No textbook is required. Module one uses Jupyter notebooks; other modules list the videos and readings assigned in the course plan.

Select Grader Than in the course menu to reach Workspace for basic Kotlin demonstrations and server examples. For general app development, you must install Android Studio on your computer. Your app assignments do not use the Grader Than autograder.

Upload a video of yourself using your app to its Canvas assignment. Use feedback to improve it, and resubmit as many times as you like until the end of class. Start early, leave time to test, and ask questions when you're stuck. I look forward to seeing what you create!
